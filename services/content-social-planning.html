Content & Social Planning

What to publish, where, how often and why — with briefs specific enough that whoever makes it doesn't have to guess what it's for.

Typical length2–4 weeks
You getCalendar & briefs
Produced byYour team or freelancer

What it is

The plan behind the posting. Most content that fails isn't badly made — it's twenty variations of the same idea, published because the calendar had a gap, testing nothing and teaching you nothing when it underperforms.

We work out what should exist, what each piece is arguing, where it runs and how often, and write briefs specific enough to produce from. We don't shoot, edit or design. You keep production wherever it already lives — in-house, a freelancer, or an agency.

What's included

Content strategy

The themes worth owning, and the ones your competitors already own.

Channel-by-channel plan

What belongs where, and what should never be cross-posted.

Editorial calendar

Cadence, formats and dates, built around what you can realistically sustain.

Creative briefs

Per piece: the claim it makes, the format, the reference, and what it has to achieve.

Hook & message bank

Angles to work through, so nobody starts from a blank page.

Production requirements

What needs shooting, writing or designing — costed enough to brief a freelancer.

How it runs

  1. Audit. What you've published, what actually performed, and what the format is costing you.
  2. Themes. The few things worth being known for, chosen against what's already claimed.
  3. Cadence. A publishing rhythm matched to the capacity you really have, not an ideal one.
  4. Brief. Each planned piece written up so production can start without a meeting.
  5. Handover. A session with whoever produces, plus the calendar in a format they'll use.

What you walk away with

  • An editorial calendar you can actually keep up with
  • A brief per planned piece, ready to hand to a producer
  • A bank of angles and hooks to work through
  • A clear list of what needs making, so you can price production accurately

Common questions

Do you produce the content itself?

No — we plan it and brief it. Photography, video, design and copywriting stay with your team or a freelancer. If you don't have anyone, the production requirements list is written so you can get accurate quotes rather than vague ones.

Can our freelancer work from these briefs?

That's exactly what they're for. Each brief names the claim, the format, the reference and what the piece has to achieve, so a producer isn't guessing at intent or waiting on approvals to start.

How far ahead does the calendar go?

Usually a quarter. Further than that and it becomes fiction — the market moves, and a calendar nobody can keep up with gets abandoned in week three. We'd rather plan twelve weeks you'll actually deliver.

What if we have almost no capacity to publish?

Then the plan says so and gets much smaller. A sustainable weekly rhythm beats an ambitious daily one abandoned by month two, and we'd rather write the plan you'll follow than the one that looks better on paper.
