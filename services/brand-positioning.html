Brand & Positioning

Before anyone can market a product, someone has to decide what it is for and what it beats. That decision is the strategy — and it is the thing most marketing budgets are quietly missing.

Typical length3–4 weeks
You getA written position
Leads intoCampaign Planning

What it is

Positioning is the choice of what you are competing with in the customer's head. Not your category on paper — the actual alternative someone weighs you against at the moment they decide. Get that wrong and every campaign afterwards argues the wrong point, expensively.

We work out the decision and write the language that carries it: who the product is for, what it replaces, why it wins, and the specific words your marketing should repeat until the market repeats them back. You get a document. What you do with it — in-house, with a freelancer, with an agency — is yours.

What's included

Market & competitor audit

Who else is claiming what, and where the language is crowded.

Customer input

Interviews, reviews and sales-call evidence for how buyers actually describe the problem.

Positioning statement

The one-paragraph decision the rest of the business can be held to.

Messaging framework

Primary message, supporting proof points, and what to say to each segment.

Tone & language guide

Words to use, words to retire, and how the brand sounds under pressure.

Rollout notes

Where the new positioning has to change first: site, ads, sales, packaging.

How it runs

  1. Audit. Your current marketing, the competitive set, and how the category talks.
  2. Evidence. Customer interviews, review mining, and whatever sales or support already knows.
  3. Options. Two or three genuinely different positions, with the trade-off of each stated plainly.
  4. Decision. We pick one together, and write the messaging framework against it.
  5. Handover. A working session so the people writing and selling can actually use it.

What you walk away with

  • A positioning document short enough that people will read it
  • A message map you can hand to a copywriter, a designer or a salesperson
  • A competitor language map showing what is already claimed
  • A prioritised list of where to apply it first

Common questions

Do we need a full rebrand?

Usually not. Positioning is a decision about meaning, not a new logo. Most of the time it changes what you say long before it changes how you look — and plenty of engagements never touch the visual identity at all.

We already have brand guidelines. Is that the same thing?

Guidelines usually cover colours, type and logo usage. That is identity. Positioning is the argument underneath it: who it's for, what it replaces, why it wins. The two work together, but one won't substitute for the other.

Who writes the actual copy afterwards?

You do, or whoever you use. We give you the message map and the language rules so a copywriter, an agency or your own team can write consistently from it. We plan and specify; we don't produce the finished assets.

Can you do this without customer research?

We can, and we'll say so plainly in the output — but it's weaker. If interviews aren't possible we lean harder on review mining, sales-call notes and search data, which is second-hand but still grounded in real language.
