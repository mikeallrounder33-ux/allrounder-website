Campaign Planning

What runs, where, when, for how much — and what each stage has to prove before the next one gets funded. This is the plan that makes the spend defensible.

Typical length4–8 weeks
Best forLaunches & seasonal pushes
Pairs withPerformance Media

What it is

A campaign plan is not a content calendar. It is the reasoning that connects a business objective to a set of channels, a budget and a date — written so that anyone on the team can see why each piece exists and what happens if it underperforms.

We build the whole thing: the objective broken into measurable stages, the audience it depends on, the channels that reach them, the money split across those channels, the creative required to fill them, and the reporting that tells you whether to keep going.

The shape of the plan changes with what you sell. We work from four campaign blueprints — product launch, D2C growth, local and retail, and B2B demand generation — and adapt whichever fits.

What's included

Objective & KPI tree

The business goal broken into stage metrics that ladder up to it.

Audience & segment map

Who we're going after first, and who we're deliberately not.

Channel mix & budget split

Where the money goes, in what order, and the logic for each share.

Offer & message architecture

What we lead with at each stage of awareness.

Creative brief

Formats, volumes, and what each asset has to accomplish.

Week-by-week calendar

Dependencies, lead times and the dates things must be finished by.

Measurement plan

What we track, where it's tracked, and the review cadence.

Risk & contingency

What we cut first if the budget moves, and the triggers for doing it.

How it runs

  1. Discover. The objective, the constraints, the existing numbers and what's been tried.
  2. Define. Audience, positioning input and the KPI each stage answers to.
  3. Design. Channel mix, budget model, offer ladder and the creative requirement.
  4. Schedule. The calendar, with production lead times worked backwards from launch.
  5. Handover or launch. Either your team runs it, or we do — see Performance Media.

What you walk away with

  • A written campaign plan with the reasoning visible, not just the schedule
  • A budget model you can change one input in and see the effect
  • A creative brief a production team can work from directly
  • A calendar with real dependencies and lead times

Common questions

How big does the budget need to be?

There's no fixed floor, but a campaign needs enough spend to learn something before it ends. If the budget is too small for the channels you have in mind, we'll say so and propose a narrower plan rather than spreading it thin across five channels.

Do you run the campaign as well as plan it?

Both are available. Some clients take the plan and run it with an in-house team; others hand the whole thing to us. The plan is written to be handed over either way, so you aren't locked in by the format of the deliverable.

How far ahead should we start?

For a launch, start at least a quarter out — creative production and lead times are what usually compress, not the strategy. For always-on work we can begin faster, since there is no fixed date to work backwards from.

What happens if the plan doesn't hit its numbers?

The measurement plan sets review points precisely so this is caught early rather than at the end. Each stage has a threshold and a stated action if it isn't met — usually reallocating budget rather than abandoning the campaign.
