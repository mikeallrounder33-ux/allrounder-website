Campaign Planning

What should run, where, when and for how much — and what each stage has to prove before the next one gets funded. Written so whoever executes it knows exactly what to do.

Typical length3–5 weeks
You getA documented plan
Executed byYour team or agency

What it is

A campaign plan is not a content calendar. It is the reasoning that connects a business objective to a set of channels, a budget and a date — written so anyone on the team can see why each piece exists and what happens if it underperforms.

We build the whole thing: the objective broken into measurable stages, the audience it depends on, the channels that reach them, the money split across those channels, the creative that needs to exist to fill them, and the reporting that tells you whether to keep going.

We plan it. You run it. The plan is written as a working brief — specific enough that your in-house marketer, your freelancer or your agency can pick it up and execute without coming back to us to interpret it.

The shape of the plan changes with what you sell. We work from four campaign blueprints — product launch, D2C growth, local and retail, and B2B demand generation — and adapt whichever fits.

What's included

Objective & KPI tree

The business goal broken into stage metrics that ladder up to it.

Audience & segment map

Who to go after first, and who to deliberately leave out.

Channel mix & budget split

Where the money goes, in what order, and the logic for each share.

Offer & message architecture

What to lead with at each stage of awareness.

Creative briefs

Formats, volumes and what each asset has to accomplish — ready to hand to a producer.

Week-by-week calendar

Dependencies, lead times and the dates things must be finished by.

Measurement plan

What to track, where, and the review cadence once it's live.

Risk & contingency

What to cut first if the budget moves, and the triggers for doing it.

How it runs

  1. Discover. The objective, the constraints, the existing numbers and what's been tried.
  2. Define. Audience, positioning input and the KPI each stage answers to.
  3. Design. Channel mix, budget model, offer ladder and the creative requirement.
  4. Document. The calendar and the briefs, with production lead times worked backwards from launch.
  5. Handover. A working session with whoever will execute, so nothing is left to interpretation.

What you walk away with

  • A written campaign plan with the reasoning visible, not just the schedule
  • A budget model you can change one input in and see the effect
  • Creative briefs a production team or freelancer can work from directly
  • A calendar with real dependencies and lead times

Common questions

Do you run the campaign as well as plan it?

No. We plan; you or your agency executes. That's deliberate — we don't take a percentage of your ad spend, so nothing in our recommendation is shaped by wanting the budget to grow. If you don't have anyone to execute, say so early and we'll write the plan so it's straightforward to hand to a freelancer or agency, and we can help you brief them.

Will our agency actually accept a plan from outside?

Usually yes, because it makes their job easier — it's written as a brief, not as a critique of their work. If you'd rather, we can walk them through it directly in the handover session so it lands as a shared starting point.

How big does the budget need to be?

There's no fixed floor, but a campaign needs enough spend to learn something before it ends. If the budget is too small for the channels you have in mind, we'll say so and plan something narrower rather than spreading it thin across five channels.

What happens if the plan doesn't hit its numbers once it's running?

The measurement plan sets review points precisely so that's caught early rather than at the end. Each stage has a threshold and a stated action if it isn't met. If you want us on hand while it runs, that's the advisory retainer rather than this engagement.
