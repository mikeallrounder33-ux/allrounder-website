What it is

The layer most businesses skip: making sure the numbers are true, then using them to decide something. Plenty of companies have dashboards. Far fewer can say which channel earned the last ten thousand dollars of revenue, or what they'd cut first if the budget dropped.

This engagement fixes the measurement, builds reporting that answers questions people actually ask, and puts a monthly decision cycle around it.

What's included

Measurement audit

What's tracked, what's double-counted, and what's silently missing.

Analytics & conversion setup

GA4, server-side events and conversion values that match the business.

Dashboard build

One view per question people actually ask, rather than every metric available.

Attribution sanity-check

Where the platform numbers disagree, and which to believe when they do.

Monthly written review

What happened, why, and the decision it points to — in plain language.

Test roadmap

The next experiments, ordered by what we'd learn against what they cost.

How it runs

  1. Audit. Everything currently tracked, and how far it is from reality.
  2. Repair. Events, values and definitions fixed at the source.
  3. Build. Dashboards structured around decisions, not around available metrics.
  4. Review. A monthly session: what the numbers say, and what changes because of it.
  5. Iterate. A rolling test roadmap so the plan keeps improving on evidence.

What you walk away with

  • Tracking you can defend in a board meeting
  • Dashboards that answer a question rather than list metrics
  • A written monthly read with a recommendation, not just a chart
  • A prioritised test roadmap for the next quarter

Common questions

We already have GA4. Do we need this?

Having GA4 and having trustworthy numbers are different things. The most common problems we find are conversion events firing on the wrong action, no revenue values attached, and platform-reported conversions being added together across channels that all claim the same sale.

What do you report on?

Whatever the business actually decides with — usually cost per acquisition, contribution margin by channel, and the leading indicators that move before revenue does. We deliberately don't report vanity metrics unless you have a specific reason to track them.

Does this replace our in-house analyst?

No. Where there's an analyst we work alongside them, usually on measurement design and the monthly decision rather than day-to-day reporting. Where there isn't one, this covers the gap without hiring for it.

Can you work with our existing tools?

Generally yes. We have no stake in which analytics or dashboard tool you use, and we'd rather work inside a stack your team already knows than migrate you onto something new for our convenience.
