What it is

A senior read on why you aren't reaching enough of the right people — and what to do about it, in order. Not a hundred-slide deck of observations: a short document that names the few things costing you the most reach and says which to fix first.

This is usually where to start. It's the fastest way to find out whether you need a campaign, a repositioning, or something that isn't marketing at all — before committing budget to any of them.

What's included

Reach & visibility audit

Where people could find you, where they can't, and what that gap is costing.

Competitor comparison

What they're doing that you aren't, and which of it is worth copying.

Channel review

What each channel is currently returning, and which are quietly subsidising the rest.

Site & conversion review

What happens to the attention you already have once it arrives.

Measurement check

Whether the numbers you're deciding from are actually true.

Prioritised roadmap

What to do, in what order, with the reasoning for the ranking.

How it runs

  1. Access. Read-only access to your analytics, ad accounts and site — we change nothing.
  2. Audit. Reach, channels, competitors, conversion and measurement accuracy.
  3. Rank. Findings ordered by impact against effort, not by how bad they look.
  4. Present. A working session where you can push back on the ranking.
  5. Roadmap. The final short document: quick wins first, then the bigger moves.

What you walk away with

  • A short written roadmap, ranked by impact against effort
  • A quick-wins list you can start on the same week
  • An honest read on which channels are worth continuing
  • A clear answer on whether the problem is the marketing at all

Common questions

Do you fix the problems you find?

Not directly — we tell you what they are, which matter most and what fixing each involves. Some are things you'll fix in an afternoon. Bigger ones usually become a campaign plan or a media plan, which are separate engagements you're free not to buy.

What access do you need?

Read-only access to your analytics, ad accounts, site and any dashboards you already have. We don't need the ability to change anything, and we won't ask for it.

What if the audit says our marketing is fine?

Then it says that, and points at whatever is actually limiting growth — pricing, the offer, the product, or the sales follow-up. That's a genuinely possible outcome and we'd rather tell you than sell you a campaign that can't fix it.

How is this different from a free agency audit?

A free audit is a sales document — its job is to find enough wrong to justify a retainer. This one is paid, short, and ranked by what would actually move your numbers, including the findings that don't lead to more work for us.
