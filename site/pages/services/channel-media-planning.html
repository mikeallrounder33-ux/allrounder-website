What it is

The decision layer above the ad account. Most wasted budget isn't wasted through bad optimisation — it's wasted by being in a channel that was never going to reach the buyer at a sane cost, or by being spread so thin across five platforms that none of them gathers enough signal to learn.

We work out where your audience is actually reachable, what each channel should get, what it's reasonable to expect back, and how the accounts should be structured. Then we write it as a brief someone else can build from.

We don't run the accounts. That keeps the recommendation clean: we have no stake in the budget going up, and you aren't tied to us to keep the plan working.

Channels we plan across

  • Meta — Facebook and Instagram, prospecting through retention
  • Google — Search, Performance Max, Shopping and YouTube
  • TikTok — where the audience and the creative can support it
  • LinkedIn — for B2B with a considered sales cycle
  • Retail & marketplace search — Amazon and equivalent
  • Organic and owned — search, email and the channels that cost time rather than media spend

We won't recommend a channel because it's fashionable. If your audience isn't reachable there at a sane cost, it doesn't go in the plan — and the plan says why.

How it runs

  1. Assess. Where your buyers are reachable, and what your existing data already proves.
  2. Model. Budget split, expected ranges, and what has to be true for each channel to pay.
  3. Specify. Account structure, targeting and the measurement each channel needs.
  4. Brief. Written so the person building it doesn't have to guess at intent.
  5. Review. A session with whoever executes, to pressure-test it before anything goes live.

Common questions

So you don't actually run our ads?

No. We decide what should run and specify how, then hand it over. Agencies typically charge a percentage of spend, which quietly rewards them for spending more of your money; we charge a flat fee for the thinking and have no stake in the number that follows.

We don't have anyone to build it. Then what?

Tell us at the start and we'll write the brief for an external team rather than an in-house one, and help you brief and choose a freelancer or agency. What we won't do is take the work on ourselves and pretend that's the same as being independent about it.

What if we have no spend history to plan from?

Then the plan starts with a deliberately small learning budget and a defined question for it to answer, rather than a confident allocation across five channels. The first version of a media plan for a new product is a test design, and we'll say so.

Can you review a plan our agency has already written?

Yes, and that's often the cheapest useful thing we do. You get a second read on the channel logic and the budget split from someone with no stake in the outcome.
